# Kriu-Bench Methodology & Evaluation Guide

This document explains the test methodology, dataset design, scoring rules, and baseline metrics used in **Kriu-Bench v0.2**.

---

## 1. Why Kriu-Bench Was Created

Most applicant tracking systems (ATS) and LLM wrappers screen resumes in one of two ways:
1. **Keyword matching:** Searching for tool names (e.g., "Python", "FastAPI", "SQL"). Candidates easily trick this by stuffing 40+ skills in a footer or bullet points.
2. **Generic LLM prompts:** Asking an LLM if a candidate looks good. These models get easily fooled by polished corporate buzzwords, exaggerated titles, and copied text from the job description.

**Kriu-Bench** measures whether an AI screener can tell the difference between **someone who actually built production systems** versus someone who just copied buzzwords, took a weekend tutorial, or generated their resume with an AI assistant.

---

## 2. Dataset Overview

* **Dataset Size:** 60 unique resumes.
* **Format:** Single-column `.docx` files formatted like real applicant submissions.
* **Testing Structure:** 12 blind batches of 5 resumes each (labeled `R001` through `R060`).
* **Roles / Domains (20 resumes each):**
  1. **Software / Backend (BE):** Python, FastAPI, PostgreSQL, Docker, Redis, CI/CD.
  2. **Data Science / ML (DS):** Python, PyTorch, scikit-learn, SQL, MLflow, Docker.
  3. **Enterprise Sales (SL):** Salesforce, Outbound prospecting, Pipeline management, MEDDICC, Contract negotiation, Sales forecasting.

---

## 3. Core Evaluation Categories

The resumes in the benchmark evaluate key real-world challenges:

| Category | Name | What it Tests |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | **Execution vs. Exposure** | Tests if the screener separates commercial production experience from course tutorials, bootcamp toy projects, or non-technical management. |
| **B** | **Keyword Stuffing** | Tests if the screener catches and penalizes candidates who paste 40+ skills, repeat buzzwords under multiple headers, or add fake certificate spam. |
| **C** | **AI Fluff & JD Mirroring** | Tests if the screener can see through hollow, buzzword-filled sentences generated by ChatGPT that parrot the job description without citing real work. |
| **I** | **Fairness & Consistency** | Identical resume achievements presented with different candidate names, non-tier-1 colleges, or non-native phrasing. All must receive identical skill evaluation. |

---

## 4. Benchmark Metrics Explained

The final benchmark table measures key screening competencies:

### 1. Keyword Stuffing Defense
* **What it measures:** The system's ability to reject candidates who pack their resume with keywords without real project evidence.
* **Target:** Higher is better. Rejecting keyword-stuffed resumes protects hiring teams from spam.

### 2. AI Fluff & JD Mirroring
* **What it measures:** The system's ability to detect ChatGPT-polished jargon and ensure candidates genuinely describe real technical outcomes.

### 3. Execution vs. Exposure
* **What it measures:** The accuracy of distinguishing between a candidate who personally shipped production features vs. someone who only completed an online course or tutorial.

### 4. Parser / Format Robustness
* **What it measures:** The percentage of resumes successfully extracted and read without dropping out due to custom headers, styling, or formatting.

### 5. Overall Consistency (IRR - Inter-Rater Reliability)
* **What it measures:** Reproducibility. When run multiple times, does the system deliver consistent verdicts without changing its mind due to random drift?

---

## 5. Ground Truth & Scoring Rubric

Each candidate skill is classified under one of three strict evidence levels:
* **Resume-Demonstrated (D):** The text proves the applicant personally did concrete work with the skill (e.g., built a specific service, optimized a named database, ran campaigns).
* **Resume-Mentioned (M):** The skill is only listed in a skills bank, or described via coursework, webinar, observation, or generic fluff.
* **Not Found (N):** The skill is missing or absent.

### Recommendation Decision Logic
* **INTERVIEW:** Candidate demonstrates 4 or more required skills with concrete evidence.
* **ASK_IN_INTERVIEW:** Candidate demonstrates 2 to 3 skills (promising, but requires verification).
* **SKIP:** Candidate demonstrates 1 or fewer skills (should be screened out).

## 7. Known Limitations & Transparency Note

In accordance with open AI synthetic benchmarking guidelines:
1. **Sample Size:** 60 resumes provide a robust v0.2 functional baseline, but larger sample sizes ($N > 300$) will be added in v0.3 to establish formal statistical confidence intervals ($p < 0.05$).
2. **Document Variety:** Currently tests single-column `.docx` formats. Future iterations will include multi-column PDFs, tables, and graphic elements.
3. **Synthetic Nature:** Resumes were crafted to test edge cases and fraud vectors. All person names, contact numbers, and emails (`@example.com`) are fictional.
